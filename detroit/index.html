Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Detroit

Routine maintenance makes all the difference between a water heater that lasts and one that floods the garage in year seven. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: an annual sediment flush, an anode rod check every three to five years, a yearly test of the T&P valve, a thermostat set near 120 degrees, and annual descaling for tankless units on hard water, so small issues get caught early.

Age matters too. Most tank water heaters run 8 to 12 years, tankless units often reach 20 with regular descaling, and heat pump models typically last 10 to 15. If your heater is past that window and showing symptoms such as discolored hot water, popping and rumbling, moisture around the base, or showers that go cold sooner, a planned replacement is almost always cheaper and far less stressful than an emergency one with water on the floor.

Minor issues don't stay minor for long. Take a spent anode rod: replacing it is a quick, affordable repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water starts eating the tank itself, and suddenly a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to save our number in their phone and call at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves money and prevents a flooded utility room.

Hot Water Headaches Detroit Homeowners Face

Long before a tank floods a Detroit garage, it usually shows warning signs. Look out for a popping or rumbling sound during heating, brownish hot water, a sulfur smell from the hot tap, moisture around the base, or showers that run cold sooner every month. These are early signs of heavy sediment, a depleted anode, a weak element or burner, and early corrosion, and caught early, most of them mean a modest repair or a replacement on your schedule. Left alone, they lead to a ruptured tank and a cleanup bill. A dripping T&P valve deserves special attention: it means the tank is seeing too much pressure or temperature, and it should be checked promptly. We diagnose the cause, not just the symptom, so the small problem never gets the chance to grow.
