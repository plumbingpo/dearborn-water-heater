Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Dearborn Heights

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which reduces the risk of scalding and slows mineral buildup. Tankless owners should plan on descaling every year if their water is hard.

The age of your heater plays a big role. With regular maintenance, a standard tank typically lasts 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts acting up, like discolored hot water, rumbling, or a damp spot around the base, it's time to plan a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule is cheaper and is far less stressful than an emergency swap with water spreading across the floor.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to high water pressure or a missing expansion tank, both modest fixes. The same drip left alone for a season can wear out the valve and stress the tank until the tank itself fails, and a small repair becomes a full replacement and a cleanup. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and ruined floors.

Hot Water Headaches Dearborn Heights Homeowners Face

Whatever type of water heater your Dearborn Heights home has, it tends to fail in a predictable way. Gas tanks usually give trouble through pilot assemblies and thermocouples, sooty burners, and draft problems in the flue. Electric units lose elements and thermostats, particularly when sediment buries the lower element. Tankless heaters throw error codes for scale buildup, ignition failures, and flow sensor trouble, most of which trace back to skipped descaling. Heat pump water heaters depend on a clean filter and a free-flowing condensate line, and they don't do well in small, closed-in spaces. We service all four types regularly and stock the common parts to finish most repairs in one trip.
