A gas water heater is a fairly simple machine, which is part of why it lasts. A burner under the tank heats the water, exhaust rises through a flue that runs up the center of the tank, and a gas control valve on the front acts as both thermostat and safety shutoff. That valve only lets gas reach the burner while it senses a flame, through a thermocouple on standing-pilot models or a flame sensor on electronic ignition models. When something goes wrong, it is usually at one of those points. In Dearborn, MI, we regularly find thermocouples that no longer hold the valve open, pilots starved by a lint-clogged flame arrestor screen, gas valves that have failed internally, and flue or vent problems that let exhaust spill back into the room.

Dearborn Water Heater does not guess with gas appliances. On every Dearborn call, our technicians test the thermocouple's millivolt output, inspect the pilot and burner flame, check gas pressure at the valve, and run a draft test at the draft hood to confirm exhaust is actually leaving the house. We look inside the combustion chamber for scorching, rust, and signs of flame rollout, which tell us whether the flue is blocked or the burner is misbehaving, and we check for carbon monoxide around the heater. One rule comes before all of it: if you smell gas, do not light anything or flip switches. Leave the house, call your gas utility from outside, and then call us once it is safe.

Most gas water heater repairs are finished in a single visit. Warning Signs to Watch For

Catching a symptom early is often the difference between a quick part swap and a tank that gives out on the garage floor. Keep an eye out for the following:

  • Showers that go cold sooner than they did a year ago
  • A pilot light that will not stay lit
  • Popping, rumbling, or crackling sounds as the burner heats
  • No hot water at all, with the burner never firing
  • Exhaust odor, rust, or moisture around the draft hood and vent pipe
  • Leaks at the drain valve, the fittings, or the bottom of the tank
  • A faint gas or rotten-egg smell near the heater (leave and call the gas utility)
  • A yellow, lazy, or sooty burner flame instead of a crisp blue one

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

Routine maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Here are our recommendations:

  • Give the heater breathing room and keep flammables well away from it
  • Drain sediment from the bottom of the tank once or twice a year
  • Check that the vent connector is secure and rising toward the chimney or flue
  • Install carbon monoxide alarms on every level of the home and test them monthly
  • Keep the thermostat around 120 degrees to save energy and reduce scalding risk
  • Check the anode rod every three to five years, since it protects the tank from corrosion
  • Keep the air intake at the bottom of the heater free of lint and dust
